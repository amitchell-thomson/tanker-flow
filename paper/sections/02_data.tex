\section{Data and pipeline}\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Sources}

Vessel positions come from three feeds with different fidelity, and the paper is
careful never to aggregate across their seams. For the United States we use the
NOAA Marine Cadastre archive of terrestrial Class-A AIS \citep{noaa_ais}, which is
exhaustive along the US coast from 2016 and ends on \noaaEnds. For Europe and the
open ocean we use Global Fishing Watch port-visit and voyage events \citep{gfw},
which supply arrivals and departures but carry no coordinates and no anchorage
events; that feed ends on \gfwEnds. A live subscription to an AIS aggregator covered
a tier-ranked fleet from April~2026 and is used only for the vintage self-validation
described in Section~\ref{sec:panel}.

Prices and controls are all public: \HH{} spot and Lower-48 storage from the EIA API
\citep{eia_api}; the TTF front-month contract from a keyless quote feed, cross-checked
against the World Bank's monthly European gas series \citep{worldbank_cmo} (slope
0.999, $\Rsq=0.9999$ over 106 months, so the continuous-roll distortion is
negligible); EUR/USD and Brent from FRED \citep{fred}; EU storage from GIE AGSI+
\citep{agsi}; and heating/cooling degree days computed from ERA5 reanalysis
temperatures at weighted demand centres \citep{openmeteo}.

\subsection{Reconstruction}

Table~\ref{tab:inventory} gives the scale of the reconstruction. Fixes are resolved
against hand-drawn berth, anchorage and approach polygons at every LNG terminal in
seven zones (US Gulf, US Atlantic, north-west Europe, Baltic, Iberia, western and
eastern Mediterranean) by a per-vessel state machine that emits zone entry,
anchorage, mooring and departure events. Three pairings of those events are the
substrate for everything that follows: a \emph{leg} pairs a departure with the
vessel's next zone entry (a voyage), a \emph{visit} pairs a mooring with the next
departure (berth occupancy), and a \emph{queue} pairs the first anchorage entry with
the next mooring at the same terminal (the wait to berth). Laden state is inferred
from reported draught against design draught.

\subsection{Ground truth}

The US leg of the reconstruction can be checked against a published total. The EIA
reports monthly US LNG exports; dividing by a nominal 174,000\,m$^3$ cargo gives an
implied cargo count, and the ratio of laden departures we observe to that count is a
capture rate (Table~\ref{tab:capture}). Restricted to the NOAA feed alone it is
\captureTwentyTwenty{} in 2020, rising to \captureTwentyThree{}, \captureTwentyFour{}
and \captureTwentyFive{} in 2023--25. The gradient is a coverage artefact of the
archive's early years, and it means early-year \emph{levels} of any US signal are
biased low by up to half; models below either work on within-window changes or
standardise, and none reads an early-year level as a physical quantity.
