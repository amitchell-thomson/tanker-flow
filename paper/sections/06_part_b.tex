\section{The spread}\label{sec:partb}

\subsection{Target, grid and controls}

The target is the spread in \$/MMBtu. Henry Hub is quoted in \$/MMBtu and TTF in
EUR/MWh, so TTF is converted at the day's exchange rate and 3.412\,MWh per MMBtu
before the difference is taken,
\begin{equation}
  \spread = \mathrm{HH}_t - \frac{\mathrm{TTF}^{\mathrm{EUR/MWh}}_t}{3.412}\,\times\,(\text{EUR/USD})_t .
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
Taking the difference before converting would leak the exchange rate into the
target as a spurious signal. The spread is sampled weekly on Mondays from 2018, the
same grid as the physical nowcasts. The scored quantity is the $h$-week-ahead change,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_h s_t = s_{t+7h} - s_t, \qquad h \in \{1, 4\},
  \label{eq:change}
\end{equation}
where $t$ is in days. The level is too persistent to score usefully, because a
model that predicts the current value performs well on it and learns nothing. The
primary sample runs to \noaaEnds{} (Section~\ref{sec:seam}) and contains
\bWeeksCov{} complete weeks.

The control block $z_t$ consists of the spread's non-tanker drivers: heating and
cooling degree days for the US and north-west Europe, US and EU storage, Brent, and
a winter dummy. The price legs that construct the target are excluded as
regressors. Fits are walk-forward with an expanding window and a 104-week minimum.
A training row is admissible only if its target closed strictly before the test
week, with a further one-week embargo \citep{lopezdeprado2018}; the purge removes
the rows whose target window straddles the test date and would otherwise carry the
future into the fit, and the embargo removes one further row as a margin. Whether
the embargo costs anything was not measured separately. Its effect on a model with
at least 104 training rows is one row in a hundred, and it protects the null and
the signals identically, so it cannot manufacture or hide a difference between them.

\paragraph{Newey--West standard errors.} On a weekly grid with $h=4$, consecutive
targets share three weeks, so the residuals are serially correlated by construction
and an ordinary least-squares standard error is roughly $\sqrt{h}$ too small. Every
standard error in this section is heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent
(HAC) in the form of \citet{neweywest1987}: with residual-weighted regressor
autocovariances $\hat{\Gamma}_j$ and lag $L = h+1$,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{V} = \hat{\Gamma}_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{L}\Bigl(1 - \frac{j}{L+1}\Bigr)\bigl(\hat{\Gamma}_j + \hat{\Gamma}_j^{\prime}\bigr),
  \label{eq:nw}
\end{equation}
and a reported $t$-statistic is $\hat{\beta}$ divided by the square root of the
corresponding element of $\hat{V}$. The lag was fixed in advance at $h+1$ weeks,
one more than the overlap, and is never chosen from the data.

\subsection{The controls-only null is worse than no change}

Three models form a ladder in which each is the null for the next:
\begin{align}
  \text{M0:}\quad & \hat{s}_{t+7h} = s_t, \label{eq:m0}\\
  \text{M1:}\quad & s_{t+7h} = a + \rho\, s_t + \varepsilon_t, \label{eq:m1}\\
  \text{M2:}\quad & s_{t+7h} = a + \rho\, s_t + \gamma^{\prime} z_t + \varepsilon_t. \label{eq:m2}
\end{align}
M0 predicts no change and is the random-walk null. M1 is an autoregression of
order one in the level, which nests M0 at $\rho=1$. M2 adds the controls and is the
AR(1)+controls null that the tanker signals were pre-registered to beat. The change
in Equation~\eqref{eq:change} is recovered from each model's level prediction.
\term{Skill} is the fractional improvement in MAE over the model's null,
\begin{equation}
  \text{skill}(m \mid \text{null}) = 1 - \frac{\mathrm{MAE}_m}{\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{null}}},
  \label{eq:skill}
\end{equation}
so that a negative skill means the model is worse than predicting no change.

Table~\ref{tab:ladder} shows that M2 does not beat M0. Relative to a no-change
forecast, M2 scores \bMTwoSkillHOneCov{} at one week and \bMTwoSkillHFourCov{} at
four. It is worse at the longer horizon and, as Figure~\ref{fig:ladderyear} shows,
worse in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025 taken individually, so the result is not a
2022 artefact. This is the behaviour expected of an efficient and persistent price
series: the level already incorporates the fundamentals, so adding them as
regressors introduces estimation noise. The operative null for the signals is
therefore M0, a stricter bar than the one pre-registered.

\subsection{No signal survives partialling}

The test for each of the eight pre-registered signals $T$ is the
Frisch--Waugh--Lovell partial effect \citep{frischwaugh1933,lovell1963}. With
$Z$ the matrix of M2's regressors (the lagged level and the controls) and
$M_Z = I - Z(Z^{\prime}Z)^{-1}Z^{\prime}$ the residual-maker, both the target and
the signal are residualised on $Z$ and the residuals regressed on each other,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{y} = M_Z\, y,\quad \tilde{T} = M_Z\, T,\quad
  \hat{\beta} = \frac{\tilde{T}^{\prime}\tilde{y}}{\tilde{T}^{\prime}\tilde{T}},
  \label{eq:fwl}
\end{equation}
with the $t$-statistic from Equation~\eqref{eq:nw}. By the theorem, $\hat{\beta}$
equals the signal's coefficient in the full regression of $y$ on $[Z, T]$, and that
equality serves as the harness's correctness test. The \pRsq{} is the \Rsq{} of the
residual-on-residual regression: the share of the variance the controls leave
unexplained that the signal explains. Partialling is not optional here. Both the
signals and the spread carry a winter cycle (gas in transit runs about
\seasonalityRatio$\times$ higher in winter, Figure~\ref{fig:seasonality}), for
unrelated reasons, and a regression of one on the other without the degree-day
controls rediscovers winter.

Each signal carried a sign fixed in advance (Appendix~\ref{app:signals}). A signal
counted as carrying information only if all three conditions held:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item $|t| > 1.96$ on the Newey--West standard error, at either horizon;
  \item the sign of $\hat{\beta}$ matched the pre-registered sign;
  \item the same sign held in at least two-thirds of individual calendar years,
        each year's partial effect being estimated on that year's weeks alone.
\end{enumerate}
The last condition guards against 2022 alone driving a pooled fit.

No signal meets the bar (Figure~\ref{fig:fwl}, Table~\ref{tab:fwl},
Figure~\ref{fig:yearsigns}). The largest $|t|$ at either horizon is \bMaxTCov, and
the largest \pRsq{} is \bMaxRsqCov. The most direct mechanical hypothesis, that
cargo at sea today lands in Europe in two to three weeks, was tested as the
EU-bound stock and gave $t=\bTransitTOneCov$ at one week and $\bTransitTFourCov$ at
four. At four weeks the four flow signals all have their pre-registered sign, but
all lie well inside the noise band. They are directionally consistent with the
mechanism and uniformly insignificant. No sign was falsified, because no estimate
reached significance.

\subsection{Three mechanisms a linear scan could miss}\label{sec:mechanisms}

A one-at-a-time linear scan has three identifiable blind spots. Each was tested with
a derived feature or a sample split, so each costs a single test. The three were
pre-registered together with a Bonferroni bar, a primary horizon of four weeks
chosen from the voyage mechanism, and a holdout (\mechHoldLoCov{} to
\mechHoldHiCov, \mechHoldNCov{} weeks) that was not examined until a hypothesis had
passed on the discovery sample (\mechDiscLoCov{} to \mechDiscHiCov, \mechDiscNCov{}
weeks). The Bonferroni bar divides the significance level by the number of primary
tests, $\alpha = 0.05/3$, so that the chance of at least one false positive across
the three stays at 5\%; the corresponding two-sided threshold is
$|t| > \bonferroniT$. A pass required the holdout sign to match.

H2 tests the share of cargo bound for Europe in place of the level,
\begin{equation}
  \text{eu\_share}_t = \frac{T^{\mathrm{eu}}_t}{T^{\mathrm{eu}}_t + T^{\mathrm{unk}}_t},
  \label{eq:h2}
\end{equation}
where $T^{\mathrm{unk}}$ is the unresolved-destination band. Most of the at-sea
stock has no resolved destination, so two collinear levels with offsetting effects
could each appear insignificant while their ratio carries the signal. A
pre-registered gate voids the test if the share is mostly a decade-long trend, which
would indicate improving coverage rather than changing cargo routing. The trend
explains $\Rsq=\mechTrendRsqCov$ and the gate passes. H3 tests an interaction with
EU storage: the regressors are $T^{\mathrm{eu}}_t$, storage $g_t$ (centred) and
their product, with the partial effect taken on the product and both main effects
moved into the control block. Additional cargo arriving when storage is 90\% full
has little effect, while the same cargo at 30\% is highly price-relevant, and a
linear fit averages the two states; the pre-registered sign on the product is
negative. H4 tests a tightness split, the simplest form of a regime model. The scan
is re-run within weeks in which EU storage is below its day-of-year seasonal norm,
with the norm fitted on the discovery sample only, and the prediction is that the
effect is positive in those weeks and larger than in the remaining weeks.

All three fail (Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}, Figure~\ref{fig:mechanisms}), and none
approaches the bar. The largest $|t|$ among the primary tests is \mechMaxTCov. Each
failure argues against a further attempt. The H2 estimate is stable and negative on
both discovery (\mechHTwoBetaCov) and holdout (\mechHTwoHoldCov), which is the wrong
sign, and it never approaches significance. H3 is positive ($t=\mechHThreeTCov$)
against a prior of negative, so neither the magnitude nor the direction of the
storage interaction is present. H4's tight-regime effect is negative
($t=\mechHFourTightTCov$) and smaller in magnitude than the loose-regime effect,
which reverses both predictions. On this evidence, cargo arrivals do not have a
measurable effect in either storage state.

\paragraph{Why they fail.} The reasons are not a defect in the data. The pipeline
is validated against the EIA total, the physical nowcasts show the signals track
the quantities they measure, and every failure above is a stable near-zero rather
than a sign that wanders. Three things explain a true null of this size. The scale
of the quantity is small: Section~\ref{sec:power} shows the EU-bound stock at sea
is about two days of European demand, so a one-standard-deviation change in it is a
day and a half of supply against a price that moves on weather revisions. The
information is not private: the marginal trader already has satellite-augmented
positions and destination inference, so public terrestrial data arrives with a
delay that the market has already discounted. And the measurement is attenuated:
three-quarters of the at-sea stock has no resolved destination, and errors in a
regressor bias its coefficient toward zero. The first two make the null the expected
answer; the third means a small real effect could be hiding under the power floor,
which is exactly what Section~\ref{sec:power} says the design cannot exclude.

The linear null of the previous subsection has thus been tested against the three
specific ways in which it could have been wrong, using pre-registered signs, a
corrected bar and an untouched holdout, and it stands.
