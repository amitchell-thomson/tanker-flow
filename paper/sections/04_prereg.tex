\section{Pre-registration protocol}\label{sec:prereg}

The project keeps a dated, append-only decision log. A hypothesis, target, lag, sign
or acceptance bar is recorded on the day its entry is written and before the
corresponding fit. A later change is made by a new, dated entry that names the
result which prompted it. The log is what allows a reader to distinguish a test that
found nothing from a search that stopped at the first result to cross a threshold.

Two consequences of the protocol appear in the results. A pre-registered sign that
the data falsified is reported as falsified (Section~\ref{sec:parta}). A test that
produced an apparent 87\% improvement over its null was withdrawn, because the
protocol's own comparability condition failed (Section~\ref{sec:parta}). Neither
outcome would have been recorded without a criterion fixed in advance. A third
consequence is smaller but real: an audit of the log before this paper was drafted
found that one synthesis entry had swapped two models' scores, and the rule that
every number in the text is generated from the replay output surfaced the
discrepancy the first time the table was read next to the prose.

\paragraph{Limits of the log.} The log is a text file in the project's version
control, and its git history does not independently establish the ordering it
asserts. The entries for the physical nowcasts (D-001 through D-026) were committed
together, weeks after the work they describe. The two spread pre-registrations
(D-028 and D-031) were each committed in the same change as their result. The
content of each pre-registration, meaning the signs, bars, horizon and holdout fixed
in its text, was written before the corresponding run, but a sceptical reader has
only the author's word for this. We state it here rather than leave it to be found.
A third-party timestamped registry would have cost nothing and will be used for any
future entry, which will be committed and pushed before the run it governs.
