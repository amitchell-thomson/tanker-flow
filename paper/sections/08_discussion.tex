\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The null is the outcome an efficient market predicts, and it is important to be
precise about which question was asked. LNG cargo tracking is a commercial industry.
Several vendors sell satellite-augmented AIS with proprietary destination inference
to gas trading desks in real time. The signals in this paper are built from public
terrestrial AIS and a free voyage archive, which is a coarser version of what the
marginal trader already has, and it reaches a public researcher with a delay that a
desk would find unusable. The test was therefore not whether the physical signal is
informative, but whether public physical data is mispriced in a liquid benchmark at
one- to four-week horizons. A negative answer to that question is close to what one
would expect in advance. What the paper adds is evidence that the negative result is
not an artefact of a defective pipeline, an unlucky specification, or a search that
stopped at the first $t>2$. The pipeline is validated against a published total,
the specification was fixed before the fit, and the search was corrected for
multiplicity and held out.

\paragraph{What the work found besides the null.} A reader who takes only the
headline would miss most of the content, so the other findings are listed here.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \emph{The naive mean is near-optimal for weekly loadings, and the reason is
    known.} Four nowcasts approached a four-week moving average from above without
    crossing it, and the one with a fitted smoothing constant chose a seven-week
    window by maximum likelihood. For a slow industrial flow that is a local level
    plus noise, the exponentially weighted mean is the optimal linear predictor, so
    this follows from theory and should temper the expectation that a richer model
    will beat a moving average on any such series.
  \item \emph{A voyage-derived feature leaks by construction unless bounded.} The
    \leakFutureShare{} of legs closed by the future at a 2020 as-of date was not a
    bug anyone wrote on purpose; it is what the natural implementation does. The
    vintage hazard is subtler still, since a date filter passes. Both are cheap to
    detect with a print log and, to our knowledge, rarely checked.
  \item \emph{Coverage has a gradient, and it runs opposite ways in the two public
    archives.} The NOAA capture rate rises from under half to above unity over the
    decade because receiver density grew; GFW's falls over the same years. Neither
    archive is a complete record on its own and the union is not their sum.
  \item \emph{A pre-registered sign was falsified.} Ballast arrivals entered the
    loadings model with a stable negative coefficient against a prior of positive.
    A ship must arrive empty before it leaves full, so the sign is a puzzle about
    timing rather than mechanism, and it is recorded rather than explained.
  \item \emph{Almost half of matured laden departures never pair with a European
    arrival.} They pair instead with the vessel's return to the Gulf, with a median
    duration of about a month. These are cargoes whose destination the public feeds
    never observed, and they are the reason the arrival model could not assume that
    a leg still open is still Europe-bound.
  \item \emph{A coverage seam was found by a plot, not a test.} The
    \coverageDropShare{} fall in the at-sea stock at the turn of 2026 passed a
    validation sweep of seventy checks and a test suite of several hundred tests.
    It was visible in the first figure drawn for this paper.
  \item \emph{An outage monitor with a stated recall came out of the exercise as the
    null, not the model.} A rate-relative silence rule detects with a median delay
    of \aFiveNTwoDelay{} days at \aFiveNTwoFar{} false alarms per terminal-year and
    \aFiveNTwoRecall{} recall.
\end{enumerate}

None of these is a price result. All of them are results about the data, and they
are the proof of concept the pipeline delivers: a decade of port events that
reconcile with a published total, built point-in-time and checked against its own
live prints. The growth in US export capacity that dominates every volume panel of
Figure~\ref{fig:signals} is real and large, but it is public information from
project sanctions and EIA reports, not something AIS reveals, and the spread scan
removes it deliberately through the persistence term.
